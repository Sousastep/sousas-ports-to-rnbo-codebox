VerbThic — Airwindows (Chris Johnson)
Page: https://www.airwindows.com/verbthic/

What it is: the sibling of VerbTiny: the same 5 controls, but the opposite
character. Where VerbTiny is thin, airy and "tssshhh" (spaciousness),
VerbThic's specially evolved matrix is dense, thick and soupy, for adding
body and weight to sounds rather than depth of space. Use it to make pads,
snares or vocals fat without buying tubes. This RNBO port uses the original
normalized 0..1 values.

Parameters (identical layout to VerbTiny, different delay network):

- p_replace (Replace) 0..1, default 0.5
  Regeneration ("Galactic-style replace" in the feedback path). This matrix
  rings denser than VerbTiny: the same values read as "heavier" and stay
  dense further up the knob. Push high for thick endless washes, watch
  saturation at the top end.

- p_derez (Derez) 0..1, default 1.0
  Bezier undersampling / darkening of the wet. Higher = cleaner; lower =
  progressively duller and more "lo-fi early reverb" character.

- p_filter (Filter) 0..1, default 1.0
  Wet tone via Bezier filter. 1.0 = brightest; lower = rounder, duller,
  like a blanket over the plate. In VerbThic it also sort of thickens when
  darkened (dark low end stays big).

- p_wider (Wider) 0..1, default 0.0
  Same tri-mode wideness as VerbTiny: normal stereo near 0, weird dual-mono
  blending near 0.5, hyper-wide opposite-phase near 1.0. On such a dense
  matrix the differences are subtler - it reads more like "thickness
  location" than space effect.

- p_drywet (Dry/Wet) 0..1, default 1.0
  Normal linear dry/wet blend (1.0 = full wet return).

Musical notes: the counterpart room-mate of VerbTiny. Use VerbThic on
things that need mass (cymbals, pads, snares) and VerbTiny for air on a
small budget. As always on Airwindows verbs, full tails develop over 2-3
seconds - trust your ears after a while, not the first 500 ms.
